---
title: Law Enforcement
---

In Viking times, justice is **decentralised**.  
With no official guards, the people must prevent crime,  
or every insult escalates into a blood feud.

In game terms consider **The People** a psuedo-NPC:

- always present in the background
- always watching, ready to intervene
- always gossiping, effectively a hive mind

The people respond to trouble by manifesting either:

- someone of high social status to demand de-escalation
- or 1d6 battle-ready folks to intervene.

The people's preferred outcomes, in order:

1. You aknowledge your crime and compensate the injured party
2. You are apprehended & brought to the next Thing for trial & sentencing (see below)
3. You are killed on the spot

## Trial & Sentencing

At the Thing, the jarl resolves outstanding disputes  
using their judgement and witness testimony.

It falls to the **victim** to enforce the sentence,  
and failure to do so is usually dismissed as weakness or cowardice.

The guilty usually recieve one of these sentences:
- pay a fine to the victim
- enter the victim's service as a thrall for a period of time
- surrender land or property to the victim
- death
- exile from the settlement (worst of all)
